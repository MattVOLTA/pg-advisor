# Assumptions behind Paul Graham's advice

Last reviewed: 2026-11-11. Review this list every quarter; it is the part of the advisor that goes stale, because the essays don't change but the world does.

Every piece of PG advice rests on assumptions about the world at the time he wrote. Before applying advice, find the assumption it rests on here and say whether it still holds.

**Basis** tells you who says the status changed. `pg-2024` / `pg-2026` means PG said so himself in an essay that year: cite it. `judgement` means this is the advisor's own reading of the present: say so in the answer ("PG hasn't written about this; my read is…").

## Company type changes the answer

Several assumptions split by what the startup spends money on. Establish this early:

- **Builds on existing models or ordinary software** (most AI apps, SaaS, marketplaces): building is cheaper than ever (P1, P3 amplified), but AI features carry per-use compute cost (P5 weakened). PG's lean-startup advice mostly applies with more force.
- **Trains or serves its own models at scale, or is capital-heavy** (foundation models, robotics, hardware, biotech): money buys speed (P2 reversed), founders depend on a few large investors (P8 split). PG's raise-as-little-as-possible logic does not transfer; his advice to start with a cheaper subset and expand still does.

## Detail

### P1. Building is cheap and getting cheaper — amplified

- **Today:** AI coding tools push the cost of building software toward the cost of describing it. This is the same trend he tracked from 2005 to 2021, only steeper.
- **What changes in the advice:** Spend little, ramen profitable and launch early all get stronger. Building something is no longer evidence of commitment or skill.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P2. Money can't make software get built faster — reversed for some startups

- **Today:** Money now buys compute and agent labour that do write software faster, and companies training models need large amounts of capital. For startups building products on top of existing models, the old premise mostly still holds.
- **What changes in the advice:** The answer to 'how much should we raise?' now depends on whether you spend on compute and models or only on people.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P3. A few people can build the whole product — amplified

- **Today:** One or two people with AI tools can now cover work that needed a team of ten in his examples.
- **What changes in the advice:** His warning not to hire too fast applies with more force. Team size stops being a signal of how serious a startup is.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P4. Great programmers are the decisive scarce input — transformed

- **Today:** Writing code is no longer scarce. What stays scarce is judgement: choosing the problem, taste, architecture, and checking what the AI produced. His language-power arguments assumed people type the code.
- **What changes in the advice:** Read the hacker essays for what they say about holding a problem in your head and caring about quality, not for advice on typing speed or picking a language.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P5. Software costs almost nothing per user — weakened

- **Today:** AI products pay real compute costs on every use, so margins look less like classic software and heavy users can lose you money.
- **What changes in the advice:** Default-alive arithmetic still works, but cost per user has to be in the model from day one.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P6. Distribution is free if the product is good — weakened

- **Today:** When anyone can build, products flood every channel and attention becomes the bottleneck. AI search and agents also change how people discover products.
- **What changes in the advice:** Doing things that don't scale and talking directly to users matter more. A good product alone spreads less reliably.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P7. A better-built product is a durable edge — weakened, and PG has said so

- **Today:** In 'The Brand Age' (2026) he argues that technology naturally erases real differences between products, after which brand takes over. In 'Making Startups Powerful' (2026) he moves the question from better product to sources of power: the customer relationship, network effects, APIs for agents.
- **What changes in the advice:** Ask what makes you powerful and hard to replace, not only what makes you better. Treat a pure technical lead as temporary.
- **Basis:** pg-2026

### P8. Founders gain leverage because startups need less capital — split

- **Today:** Founders of lean application companies have more leverage than ever. Founders of capital-heavy AI companies depend on a few very large investors.
- **What changes in the advice:** Before applying his fundraising advice, work out which kind of company you are.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P9. Investors herd and follow momentum — holds

- **Today:** This is human behaviour, and AI hype cycles if anything show it more clearly.
- **What changes in the advice:** None. Advice to raise in parallel and treat anything short of yes as no stays good.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P10. VC fund economics shape investor behaviour — holds

- **Today:** Returns still come from a few huge winners. Fund sizes and round sizes have changed, but the incentives work the same way.
- **What changes in the advice:** The logic carries over. The specific numbers do not.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P11. Specifics of a particular period — dated

- **Today:** Sarbanes-Oxley, super-angels, typical round sizes, Microsoft's dominance and the other period details are history, whatever AI does.
- **What changes in the advice:** Use these as examples of reasoning, never as current facts.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P12. Human nature — holds

- **Today:** Determination, conformity, curiosity, procrastination and status-seeking have not changed.
- **What changes in the advice:** None. These are his most durable essays.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P13. Arithmetic and logic — holds

- **Today:** Compounding, his equity test and default-alive maths are formulas. The formulas hold, but the numbers you put into them have changed.
- **What changes in the advice:** Keep the formulas and refresh the inputs, such as growth benchmarks and cost per user.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P14. Talent and capital cluster in places — holds, contested

- **Today:** Remote work lowered the bar to building from anywhere, but AI talent and capital concentrated heavily in a few cities.
- **What changes in the advice:** Mostly holds. Being near the frontier still matters.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P15. Big organizations are slow and lose the best people — split

- **Today:** Small teams have more leverage than ever. But frontier AI work needs compute, data and distribution that incumbents already have, so some big companies have become more dangerous competitors.
- **What changes in the advice:** Don't assume a big company will be too slow to compete with you when the work depends on compute and data.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P16. Proxies and tests get gamed; real performance matters — amplified

- **Today:** AI makes essays, exams and portfolios trivially easy to game, so proxies tell you less and less.
- **What changes in the advice:** Judge people and progress by real work and users, which is his advice already, now with more force.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P17. Writing is how people think — updated by PG

- **Today:** In 'Writes and Write-Nots' (2024) he says AI has removed the pressure to write, so people will split into those who can write, and therefore think, and those who can't.
- **What changes in the advice:** His view hasn't changed but the stakes have. Writing is now a deliberate choice, and he expects it to separate people.
- **Basis:** pg-2024

### P18. Specific technology predictions — resolved

- **Today:** Most of these have played out, e.g. web apps, tablets, the decline of Microsoft's power.
- **What changes in the advice:** Treat them as a track record, not as guidance.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P19. Technology makes pleasures more addictive — amplified

- **Today:** AI companions and endlessly generated feeds are the next step in the concentration he described in 2010.
- **What changes in the advice:** His advice gets stronger: deliberately defining yourself by what you refuse.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P20. Cheaper failure means more attempts — amplified

- **Today:** The cost of an attempt is lower than ever, so more startups get tried and copies appear faster.
- **What changes in the advice:** Expect many competitors fast. Speed and power matter more than having the idea first.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P21. Taste is real and learnable — holds, more valuable

- **Today:** When generating things is nearly free, choosing what is good becomes the scarce skill.
- **What changes in the advice:** Taste moves from a craft skill to a central skill for founders.
- **Basis:** judgement

### P22. Users decide — holds

- **Today:** This still holds, with a new wrinkle he names himself in 2026: some of your users may now be AI agents.
- **What changes in the advice:** Make what people want, and also make it usable by the agents acting for them.
- **Basis:** judgement and pg-2026
